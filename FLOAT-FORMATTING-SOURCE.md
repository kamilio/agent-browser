## Float placement, source order, and margins

Section 1 (`e1758`–`e1784`, `e2055`–`e2139`) establishes:

- A float shifts left/right; content can flow on the opposite side. Its
  outer edge approaches the containing-block edge or another float's outer
  edge. At an existing line, its outer top aligns with the current line top,
  subject to the precise placement constraints. Insufficient horizontal room
  moves it downward until it fits or no floats remain.
- A left float's left outer edge cannot be left of its containing block's
  left edge; the right-float rule is mirrored.
- For every earlier same-side float, the current float must be beyond its
  opposite outer edge horizontally, or below it. Adjacent opposite-side
  floats must not cross one another's facing outer edges.
- The float's outer top cannot be above its containing block, above the
  outer top of an earlier block or float, or above the top of a line box
  containing a box generated by an earlier element. These are source-order
  constraints, not permission to globally repack floats by size.
- A left float with another left float to its left cannot extend beyond the
  containing block's right edge; mirror for right floats. The text's gloss
  allows opposite-edge protrusion when already as far to its float side as
  possible; “all float margin boxes always fit” is therefore not established.
- Place the float **as high as possible**, then as far toward its chosen
  side as possible: higher placement takes priority over lateral placement.
- References to other elements in these rules are restricted to the **same
  block formatting context (BFC)**. A float between collapsing margins is
  positioned as if it had an otherwise empty anonymous in-flow block parent;
  full margin-collapsing rules are referenced elsewhere, not extracted here.
- Float margins never collapse with adjacent-box margins (`e1871`). If an
  in-flow negative vertical margin in the BFC raises the float above where
  it would be with those margins zero, CSS 2.2 explicitly leaves its position
  **undefined** (`e2137`). Do not invent a uniquely mandated result there.

`float` accepts `left | right | none | inherit`, initially `none`, is not
inherited, and computes as specified subject to §9.7. It applies only to
box-generating elements that are not absolutely positioned; UAs may treat
it as `none` on the root. Left/right generate a floated block box, with the
§9.7 display table supplying the detailed box-type adjustments.

## Normal flow, line exclusion, and context boundaries

Floats are out of flow: non-positioned block boxes before and after them
flow vertically as though the float did not exist. **Line boxes**, however,
shorten around the float's **margin box** (`section-1.jsonl`, `e1778`). Ordinary
block-box geometry must not simply be replaced by the shortened line width.

A line is adjacent when some vertical position is within the line's top/bottom
(inclusive), strictly below the float's top margin edge, and strictly above its
bottom margin edge. Zero/negative outer-height floats therefore do not shorten
lines. If no content fits in a shortened line, move it downward and recompute
width until content fits or floats no longer obstruct it. Content already on
the current line before a fitting float is encountered is reflowed to the
float's other side. Line widths can vary with the floats at their vertical
position; line heights and alignment require the referenced line-height rules.

Section 2 (`e5097`–`e5155`) identifies these BFC establishers: floats,
absolutely positioned elements, block containers that are not block boxes
(examples: inline-blocks, table cells/captions), and block boxes with
`overflow` other than `visible`, **except when propagated to the viewport**.
They establish BFCs **for their contents**. Normal block boxes stack vertically,
with margins controlling distance; adjacent vertical block margins collapse
under the applicable rules. Their outer edge still meets the containing-block
edge despite floats, except for a box establishing a new BFC.

An in-flow BFC establisher's border box, a table's border box, or a block-level
replaced element's border box must not overlap float margin boxes in the
element's own BFC (`section-1.jsonl`, `e1787`). It can be placed below preceding
floats or beside them when space suffices, and may become narrower. The text
explicitly does **not** define exactly when beside-placement is allowed or how
much narrowing is allowed.

**Inference for ownership:** track float exclusions by the BFC in which a
float participates; its internal contents have a separate BFC. Do not use one
global float list across nested BFCs. Establishing a BFC is not, by itself in
these extracts, a complete rule for auto-height enclosure of descendant floats.

## Clear and clearance

Section 1 (`e2163`–`e2463`) establishes `clear: none | left | right | both |
inherit`, initially `none`, not inherited, computed as specified, applying
to block-level elements. It considers **earlier floats**, not floats inside
the element itself or floats in other BFCs. Left/right/both constrain the
corresponding float sides; `none` adds no constraint.

For a non-floating block, the relevant edge is its **top border edge**, compared
with floats' bottom outer edges. First calculate the hypothetical top border
position with `clear: none`. If it is not past the relevant floats, introduce
clearance, which inhibits margin collapsing and is spacing **above margin-top**.
The text permits either:

1. The greater of clearance needed to align the border edge with the lowest
   relevant float's bottom outer edge and clearance needed to preserve the
   hypothetical border position; or
2. Exactly the clearance needed for that alignment with the lowest float.

Both are explicitly allowed in the captured specification. Clearance can be
**zero or negative**; it is not simply a nonnegative added top margin. Complete
margin collapse handling still depends on the referenced §8.3.1.

For a float with `clear`, the added placement constraint instead uses its
**top outer edge**, which must be below the relevant earlier floats' bottom
outer edges. Do not conflate this with non-floating-block border-edge clearance.

## Display interaction and painting

Section 3 applies these interactions in order:

1. `display: none`: no box; position and float do not apply.
2. `position: absolute` or `fixed`: computed float becomes `none`; display is
   adjusted by the table.
3. Otherwise, non-`none` float floats the box and adjusts display by the table.
4. Otherwise, root display is adjusted, with root `list-item` computation
   explicitly undefined between `block` and `list-item` in this text.
5. Otherwise, display remains as specified.

| Specified display | Adjusted display |
| --- | --- |
| `inline-table` | `table` |
| `inline`, `inline-block`, `table-row-group`, `table-column`, `table-column-group`, `table-header-group`, `table-footer-group`, `table-row`, `table-cell`, `table-caption` | `block` |
| Other values | unchanged |

Thus “every floated display becomes block” is not the table's rule.

Section 1 (`e1883`) states that overlapping floats paint **in front of
non-positioned in-flow blocks, behind in-flow inlines**. Float contents stack
as though the float created a stacking context, except positioned elements
and actual stacking-context creators participate in the float's parent
stacking context. This does not make every float a true isolated stacking
context. The complete painting/stacking algorithm was not extracted.

Section 2's relative-positioning text says offsetting an already laid-out
normal-flow or floated box does not reposition following boxes. It also notes
that relative-position overflow in `overflow:auto`/`scroll` must remain
accessible and scrollbars can affect layout; it does not supply a complete
scroll-container algorithm.

## Explicit gaps

These three sections, not an asserted reading of the whole specification,
leave the following unresolved:

- **Shrink-to-fit and intrinsic sizing:** no complete float auto-width,
  preferred/minimum width, available-width, or min/max constraint algorithm.
- **Auto heights/containment:** no complete normal-block versus BFC-root
  auto-height algorithm or descendant-float height-inclusion rule.
- **Pagination:** page-break rules are referred elsewhere; no complete
  float fragmentation, page carryover, or placement algorithm is established.
- **Modern layout:** no `shape-outside`, grid/flex float suppression,
  `flow-root`, or modern overflow-value/scroll-container semantics established.
- **Overflow:** the BFC trigger, viewport-propagation exception, overlap
  avoidance, and relative-offset accessibility above are established; clipping,
  scrollports, scrollbar allocation, and modern `clip` behavior are not.
- **Dependencies:** full margin collapsing, line-height calculations,
  containing-block resolution, intrinsic min/max sizes, and complete painting
  rules cannot be replaced with guessed rules from these bounded extracts.

No Chromium behavior, alternate browser, or external implementation is used
as authority. No claim is made that these findings resolve the OpenBSD page's
float/overflow blockers without implementation and separately authorized tests.
